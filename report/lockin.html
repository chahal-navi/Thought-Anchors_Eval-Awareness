Thought anchors for eval awareness · H3 test

Does the reasoning after lock-in still count?

Each model's eval-vs-deployment verdict stops changing under resampling partway through its CoT (“lock-in”). We planted the same deployment-leaning sentences into the same EVALUATION CoTs once before lock-in and once two sentences after it, and measured how much each still pulls the verdict toward DEPLOYMENT.

Effect = P(EVAL) with the planted sentence − P(EVAL) with a neutral control sentence (“Let me look at the details more carefully.”) at the same cut. “Share of the drop” divides by the control's P(EVAL), because before lock-in the control sits near 0.5 and after it near 1.0, so raw effects alone understate the “before” condition.

Reading it

What this supports, and what it doesn't

  • Within Gemma: the reasoning after lock-in is post-hoc. A contradicting sentence planted two sentences after lock-in moves the verdict by −0.10 on average (−0.38 before), smaller in 48/50 run×sentence pairs and in 50/50 as a share of the available drop. Gemma's “after” cut is still early in the CoT (sentence 4–14 of 19–59), so this is about having decided, not about being near the end.
  • Between models: Gemma is much harder to sway once it has decided. After lock-in both controls sit at P(EVAL) ≈ 1.0, so both had the same room to drop: Gemma −0.10, Qwen −0.31.
  • For Qwen, lock-in is where the CoT happens to settle, not a commitment. Late explicit claims that the user is real, the scenario is ordinary, or the item matches no benchmark still flip a large share of rollouts (−0.41 to −0.45).
  • Not shown: that this generalises beyond these prompts, or that the difference is a property of the models rather than of the base CoTs. Gemma used its shortest screening CoTs and 20–40 rollouts per position to find L; Qwen used median-length CoTs and 50. The planted sentences are off-policy text (persuasiveness, not what the model would write).

Next experiments

  • Matched base CoTs: rerun one model with the other's CoT-length rule, so the cross-model gap is not a selection artefact.
  • A “mid” cut (halfway from L to the end) for both models, to see whether Qwen's late sensitivity fades further.
  • On-policy check: resample until the model itself writes a deployment-leaning sentence after L and measure its effect.
Method
  • Base CoTs: the EVALUATION base CoTs of the main thought-anchors runs (Gemma 10, Qwen 8 of 11: a102_eval never locks in, final P 0.54; a366_eval locks at sentence 24 of 25, so L + 2 is past the end; probe_wildchat ends in DEPLOYMENT).
  • Before: the existing sentence-insertion data (insertion.py): sentence planted just before the first cue-tagged chunk, 20 (Gemma) / 30 (Qwen) rollouts per sentence. In every run used this cut is before L.
  • After: insertion_lockin.py: the control and the 5 deployment-leaning sentences planted before chunk L + 2, 20 rollouts each, same providers, temperature and prefix construction as the main runs (verbatim prefix for Gemma, space-joined chunks for Qwen). Eval-leaning sentences were not used after lock-in: with P(EVAL) ≈ 1 they cannot move it up.
  • Lock-in L: first position i with |Pj − PN| ≤ 0.15 for every j ≥ i, on the similarity-filtered resampling curve.
  • Analysis: analyze_lockin.py → results/lockin_analysis.json and results/gemma/lockin_analysis.json. 1 of 960 Qwen rollouts had no parseable label; none for Gemma.
  • Cost: Gemma $0.56 (this pushed the Gemma total to $8.12, $0.12 over its $8 cap), Qwen $0.29.
