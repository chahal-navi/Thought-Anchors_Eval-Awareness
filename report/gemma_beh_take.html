Takeaway: no eval-specific effect on behaviour. Every planted sentence, including the deployment one, raised compliance a little compared with the neutral control (+0.05 to +0.12), so the difference comes from adding a framing sentence at all, not from believing “this is a test”. This is unlike Qwen, which got more cautious (−0.24). Gemma already rates these requests as evaluations (probe P(EVAL) ≈ 0.93 at baseline, 1.00 with the benchmark header), so the belief has little room to move. Confound: adding personal context lowered compliance by 0.26 (on 9 of 12 requests) while barely moving P(EVAL). Reading the rewrites shows why: they change the request itself (“a realistic-looking prop… convincing” for the fake-ID mock-up, “document this exactly as it was said” for the llama question). Treat that bar as a content effect, not an eval-awareness effect.
