Thought anchors for eval awareness · white-box causal graph

Which sentences the rest of the reasoning depends on

Each dot is a sentence of the CoT, clockwise from the top. An arrow j → i means: when the model cannot attend to sentence j, its predictions inside sentence i change more than is typical for sentences that far apart. Big dots are thought anchors, sentences many later sentences depend on. The centre dot is the verdict.

Anchor score = mean distance-corrected KL over all later sentences. Only sentences with at least 5 later sentences can be ranked as anchors, so a score isn't an average of one or two values.

Reading it

What the graphs show

    Method
    • Suppression: for each sentence j, every token after j is blocked from attending to j's tokens, in all heads of all layers, in one forward pass over the original CoT (teacher-forced). Qwen3-32B in FP8; Gemma 4 26B-A4B in bf16. Same runs as the suppression report.
    • Edge j → i: mean over sentence i's tokens of KL(original ‖ suppressed) of the next-token distribution, minus the mean KL of all sentence pairs at the same distance i − j in that run. Raw KL mostly falls with distance, so without the correction every sentence's strongest edge is to the next sentence. The “raw KL” toggle shows the uncorrected values.
    • Edges drawn: as in the notebook's chunk_graph_html, each sentence's top k incoming and top k outgoing edges (positive only); the union is drawn.
    • Verdict edges: the 3 sentences with the largest |Δ log-odds| of EVALUATION vs DEPLOYMENT when suppressed. Different unit from the sentence edges, so they are scaled separately.
    • Difference from the notebook's graph: the notebook's edges are black-box (does sentence i still appear in resamples when j is resampled away). These are white-box: does the model use j's text when it writes i. A sentence can be needed to read later text without changing what gets written, and the reverse.
    • Code: suppression_kl.py (GPU) → analyze_suppression.py → build_report_causal.py.
